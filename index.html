Privacy Policy

The Scratch Project · Last updated November 15, 2026

The Scratch Project is a golf app for logging rounds, practice and notes. It is built so that your information stays on your own phone. This policy explains what that means in practice.

What We Collect

Only what you choose to share, described below: green locations, if you turn that sharing on, and a drill or practice plan, each time you tap Share on one. The app has no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no crash reporting service. Apart from what you share, nothing you enter is sent to us or to anyone else.

Network Requests

The app makes up to three kinds of network request:

  • Map imagery. When you open the hole map, it fetches satellite imagery tiles for that hole from Esri (ArcGIS Location Platform). That request tells Esri which patch of ground to draw, which reveals roughly where you are playing, and includes nothing else about you. Tiles are kept on your phone for about a week so a hole you have viewed works without a connection. Esri's own privacy policy covers how they handle those requests.
  • Shared green locations, only if you have turned sharing on. See below.
  • Shared drills and plans, only when you tap Share on one or enter a code under Import From Code. See below.

If you never open the hole map and never share anything, the app makes no network connections at all, apart from the app store handling a purchase (see Purchases).

Shared Green Locations

The hole map lets you place a green's center, front and back on a course you picked from the app's course list. The first time you do, the app asks whether to share green locations. Nothing is sent unless you choose Share, and you can turn sharing on or off at any time under Backup & Restore. Turning it off also discards any locations still waiting to be sent.

When sharing is on, each green point you place is sent with:

  • which course and hole it belongs to, and whether it is the center, front or back;
  • its map coordinates;
  • whether you placed it on the map or at your own position, and, for your own position, how accurate the phone's location fix was;
  • a random identifier created by the app on your phone, and the time it arrived.

The random identifier is not tied to your name, your account, your phone number or your device's advertising ID. It lets us count how many different phones agree on a green, so that no single phone can move one, and limit how many points one phone can send in a day. A point you place at your own position does reveal that your phone was standing on that green at that moment; a point placed on the map does not say where you were.

Shared points are stored by our database host, Supabase, in the United States. Like any internet request, sending them reveals your phone's IP address to that host; we do not store it with the points. We use the points only to improve the green positions in the app's course list: where at least three phones agree on a green, its position is included in a future version of the app. Shared points are never sold and never used for advertising.

Because shared points carry no name or account, we cannot tell which points are yours, so we cannot find and delete them on request. Turning sharing off stops any more from being sent.

Shared Drills and Plans

You can share a drill or a practice plan with a friend. When you tap Share, the app sends the drill's words and settings (its name, instructions, part of the game, type, focus areas and equipment), or a plan's name, settings and the drills in it, together with the same kind of random identifier described above. Photos are never sent. The app then shows a short code, which you pass on however you like.

Anyone who enters that code under Import From Code can add a copy to their own app for the next 90 days, after which the code stops working. Shares are stored by Supabase in the United States, cannot be browsed or searched, and are found only by their exact code. The random identifier is used only to limit how many shares one phone can make in a day. Anything in a shared drill or plan is visible to whoever has its code, so leave out anything you would not want passed on. Entering a code sends only that code. A share can be reported from the import screen, which hides it from everyone. To have one removed sooner, email us its code.

What the App Stores on Your Phone

Everything you enter stays in the app's private storage on your device: rounds and scores, practice drills and sessions, yardages, routines, journal entries, settings, and any photos or swing videos you record or import. We cannot see any of it.

If the app crashes, it saves a short technical report on your device. You can view, save or delete these reports under Backup & Restore. They are only shared if you choose to send one yourself.

Camera, Microphone and Photos

The app asks for the camera to take photos for journal entries and drills and to record swings for the Swing Analyzer. It asks for the microphone so swing recordings include sound, which lets you hear impact when you review them. It can save an annotated swing frame to your photo library if you ask it to. Photos, videos and recordings stay on your device.

Location

The app asks for your precise location to show yardages to the green on the scorecard. Your position is compared with the green on your own course card and shown as a number; it is not recorded and it is not sent anywhere. If you use Center From My Position in the hole map's marker editor, the one position you were standing at is saved into that course card on your phone, and, only if you share green locations, sent as described above. You can decline the permission and the scorecard works without yardages.

Purchases

Pro is a one-time purchase made through Google Play or the App Store. The store handles the payment under its own terms; the app only asks the store whether Pro is unlocked and keeps no record of the purchase itself. We never see your payment details.

Backups

You can export a backup file from Backup & Restore. Where that file goes is up to you.

Your phone's own backup service (Google backup on Android, iCloud on iPhone) may include the app's data as part of your regular device backup, stored in your own account under Google's or Apple's terms. On Android, that cloud backup includes the app's database and settings but not swing videos or photos. You can turn device backup off in your phone's settings.

Sharing

We do not sell any data or use it for advertising. Anything that leaves your phone, such as a backup file, a crash report, a saved image, a shared drill or plan or a shared green location, leaves only because you chose to send, save or share it. The one exception is the hole map's imagery request described above, which goes to Esri and carries only the map area to draw.

Deleting Your Data

Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on your device. Backup files you exported and device backups are kept until you delete them. Shared green locations stay in our database to keep improving the course list; they cannot be traced back to you. Shared drills and plans are kept for 90 days from when you shared them.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. The only information it can collect from anyone is the shared green locations, drills and plans described above, which carry no name or account.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
